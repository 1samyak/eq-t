01 / The research gap

The data is public.
The work is not.

Investors can find annual reports, exchange filings and earnings calls. The hard part is connecting them, checking what the numbers imply, and building a view that can be defended.

01 — FRAGMENTED

Evidence lives everywhere.

Filings, transcripts, statements and disclosures are spread across formats and sources.

02 — SHALLOW

Ratios rarely tell the whole story.

A metric without context can hide changes in cash quality, working capital or accounting choices.

03 — UNCONNECTED

Contradictions are easy to miss.

The signal may sit between reported profit, operating cash flow, receivables and management commentary.

05 / The standard

A claim is only as useful
as the evidence behind it.

EQ-T’s intended edge is not a confident answer. It is a repeatable way to move from a question to supporting evidence, cross-checks and a reasoned conclusion.

The evidence principle

Show the source. Show the period. Show the limitation.

Every research output should make it possible to inspect where a statement came from, what it was compared against and what still needs analyst review.

Source referencePeriod alignmentCross-checksCounter-thesis
01 / Not a black box

Evidence before assertion.

Separate reported facts, calculated metrics, model-generated interpretation and analyst judgement.

02 / Not a verdict machine

Flags are questions, not accusations.

An accounting indicator should trigger investigation, not be presented as proof of misconduct.

03 / Not a substitute

Analyst stays in control.

EQ-T is built to support due diligence and independent reasoning — not replace it.
